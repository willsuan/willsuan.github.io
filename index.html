    § 02 / research direction

    Where I want to do work.

    I am drawn to questions about how structure in neural population activity supports computation. Each interest below opens a short note on what specifically pulls me toward it.

    i01 neural population structure

    How do the joint firing patterns of many neurons carry computation? I am interested in how covariance structure, low-dimensional shared variability, and population geometry constrain what a circuit can represent and how flexibly it can recombine those representations.

    i02 latent dynamics

    The view that neural activity traces low-dimensional trajectories in state space — and that those trajectories implement the computation — is the framing I find most generative. I want to get more careful at fitting, validating, and interpreting latent dynamical models on real recordings.

    i03 representation geometry

    Geometry as a vocabulary for what a representation does: which distinctions are separable, which are tangled, how curvature and dimensionality track task structure. I am drawn to the parts of this work that connect cleanly to differential geometry and to concrete measurements you can make on data.

    i04 statistical models for neural data

    Point-process models, state-space models, hierarchical and generative latent-variable models — the toolkit that lets us actually say something about noisy, partially observed neural recordings. I care about identifiability, finite-sample behaviour, and what a fit really tells you.

    i05 biological & artificial representations

    Comparing what brains and trained networks encode — not as a metaphor, but as a measurement problem. RSA, CKA, alignment, and the careful follow-ups that distinguish a real structural match from a coincidence of the metric.

    i06 scalable methods for high-d data

    The computational side: numerical linear algebra, tensor methods, GPU-accelerated fits, careful evaluation pipelines. The kind of work that decides whether a method actually runs on a real dataset or stays a toy demo.

    § 03 / research statement

    When an idea takes shape in the brain, what shape does it take?

    Shape is meant literally. The joint activity of a neural population traces something with geometry, including distances between states, curvature of trajectories, and the dimensionality a circuit is willing to use, and that geometry seems to carry the work of representation. The question I keep coming back to is whether distances on a population manifold correspond to discriminability between stimuli, and what that would say about how the brain encodes them.

    What carries weight in this area is rarely the headline method. A latent-variable fit or an alignment metric is only as trustworthy as the parameter sweeps, finite-sample checks, and figures around it. Working on structure-preserving networks taught me a version of the same lesson, that the geometry you bake into a model is not the geometry it ends up using, because training deforms it and the model finds another way. The same caution belongs in neuroscience.

    I'm comfortable across the stack this work needs. I've built controlled Hamiltonian optimization modules and port-Hamiltonian forecasting architectures in PyTorch, fit point-process and state-space models to spiking data with MCMC, run MPI-parallelized FDTD sweeps in C++, and shipped detection and tracking pipelines with the evaluation work to back them up. I can take a question end to end, from numerical methods and model fitting through statistical evaluation, ablations, and the kind of careful figures that turn a demonstration into evidence.
